# Federal TRIO and GEAR UP: published funding levels (FY2021–FY2026) and competition cycles

Researched 2026-10-14. All ed.gov pages were read on 2026-10-14 in a real browser. ed.gov sits behind an Azure WAF JavaScript challenge, so `curl` and other non-browser fetches get HTTP 403; plan for this if you automate it. Federal Register, govinfo, grants.gov and CourtListener documents were fetched directly.

## Summary

- **Appropriations (ED budget tables and the FY2026 explanatory statement, in thousands of dollars).** Federal TRIO Programs: 1,097,000 (FY2021), 1,137,000 (FY2022), 1,191,000 (FY2023), 1,191,000 (FY2024), 1,191,000 (FY2025), 1,191,000 (FY2026). GEAR UP: 368,000, 378,000, 388,000, 388,000, 388,000, 388,000 for the same years.
- **FY2025** was funded by the Full-Year Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2025 (P.L. 119-4, Mar. 15, 2025). ED's FY2027 budget table labels the FY2025 column "2025 Final Operating Plan".
- **FY2026** was enacted in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (P.L. 119-75, Feb. 3, 2026). Its "Final Bill" table, printed in the Congressional Record on Jan. 22, 2026, shows TRIO 1,191,000 and GEAR UP 388,000.
- **President's budget requests.** Both the FY2026 and FY2027 requests proposed **0** for TRIO and for GEAR UP. Congress did not adopt this for FY2026.
- **ED program pages.** ED's TRIO pages publish allocation, number of awards and average award only through **FY2024**. For FY2025 they publish project counts only. GEAR UP pages split new and continuation funding only through **FY2023**.
- **Project periods.** TRIO grants (SSS, TS, UB, UBMS, VUB, EOC, McNair) run up to 60 months. GEAR UP grants run 72 or 84 months.
- **Competition timing.**
  - SSS was competed in FY2020 and FY2025; ED says the next is FY2030.
  - TS and EOC were competed in FY2021 and again in FY2026. The FY2026 competitions were run by the Department of Labor's Employment and Training Administration (DOL ETA) on ED's behalf.
  - UB, UBMS, VUB and McNair were last competed in FY2022. Their next competition is FY2027 by inference only: no FY2027 notice was found as of 2026-10-14.
  - GEAR UP has competitions most years: FY2021 (State and Partnership), FY2023 (State), FY2024 (both) and FY2025 (both).
- **2025–2026 disruptions that will show up in USAspending:**
  - Summer/September 2025 non-continuations of some TRIO and GEAR UP grants, plus FY2025 SSS non-selections. A federal court vacated these for members of the Council for Opportunity in Education (COE) on Jan. 16, 2026.
  - FY2025 project counts fell for every TRIO program except SSS.
  - Since Nov. 2025, an ED–DOL interagency agreement has DOL administering most HEA postsecondary grant programs.
  - The FY2026 TS and EOC notices allow much larger maximum awards, and ED estimates far fewer awards (TS about 175, EOC about 55).

## 1. Funding table

Units are given in each row. ED budget tables are **in thousands of dollars**. ED program pages and Federal Register notices give **whole dollars**. "Type" values:
- **approp**: enacted appropriation.
- **request**: President's budget request.
- **program alloc.**: ED's program-level "Funding Allocation". This combines new and continuation awards and does not split them. The note in parentheses says whether that year was a new-award (competition) year or a continuation year. That label is *my inference* from the competition calendar in section 2.
- **new** / **continuation**: ED's own split, published only for GEAR UP.

### How well can renewal timing be inferred from award start/end dates alone?

**Reasonably well for TRIO, with caveats. Less well for GEAR UP.**

- **TRIO.** Each program competes its whole national cohort at once on a roughly 5-year rhythm, so a large cluster of new awards sharing one start date identifies the competition year. The next competition is about 60 months later: SSS FY2020→FY2025→FY2030; TS/EOC FY2021→FY2026; UB/UBMS/VUB/McNair FY2022→FY2027.
- **Caveats for TRIO:**
  1. ED sometimes makes later awards "from the list of unfunded applications from this competition" (stated in every NIA). These start off-cycle but belong to the same cohort. Award counts rose without a new competition, e.g., TS 530→551 and EOC 170→183 from FY2021 to FY2022.
  2. Non-continuations in 2025 ended some projects early. The end dates recorded in USAspending may still show the original project period, or may be shortened by a modification (UNVERIFIED which).
  3. No-cost extensions can push end dates out.
  4. FY2026 TS and EOC awards were run by DOL ETA. Their awarding-agency and sub-agency fields, and their ALN, may differ from earlier cohorts (UNVERIFIED until the awards appear).
  5. The FY2026 TS and EOC designs differ sharply from earlier cohorts: governor-designated state-level applicants with maximum awards of $10,000,000 (TS) and $3,000,000 (EOC), and an estimated 175 TS and 55 EOC awards. Award counts and sizes will not be comparable to the FY2021 cohort.
- **GEAR UP.** Cohorts start nearly every year and last 72 or 84 months, so end dates alone cannot tell you when the "next competition" is. They only tell you when individual grants expire. Competition years have to come from the Federal Register.
- **Also worth checking in the data (UNVERIFIED).** ED award IDs (FAINs) usually embed the competition fiscal year as two digits (for example `P042A20xxxx` for FY2020 SSS). If that holds, it is a stronger signal of cohort than the start date. Verify against USAspending records before relying on it.
