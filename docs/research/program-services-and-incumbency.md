# TRIO and GEAR UP: required and permissible services, vendor contracting, incumbency advantage, and buying windows

Researched 2026-10-14. This note builds on `trio-gearup-funding-and-cycles.md` and does not repeat its funding and competition-calendar material.

**Sources.**
- Statute: Cornell LII (law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/...). uscode.house.gov returned "Page Not Found" for these sections on 2026-10-14 because the site is "operating in a temporary format".
- Regulations: eCFR versioner API, Title 34 and Title 2, as of 2026-10-13. eCFR reports Title 34 is "up to date as of 2026-10-13".
- Notices: federalregister.gov.
- ed.gov was not used (it blocks automated access).
- One section (buying windows) also uses USAspending award records as data. These are labelled as observations, not law.

**Tags used for the company's offerings:**
- **[TP]** test or college-entrance-exam prep
- **[TU]** tutoring
- **[EN]** academic enrichment
- **[CU]** curriculum
- **[ST]** staff training or professional development
- **[WF]** workforce, career, or reentry readiness

## Summary

- **Test prep and tutoring are required by statute in three TRIO programs.**
  - Talent Search (TS) and Upward Bound (UB, which includes UBMS and VUB) must provide "assistance in preparing for college entrance examinations". UB must also provide academic tutoring. TS must provide "connections to high quality academic tutoring services".
  - The TS and Educational Opportunity Centers (EOC) regulations list "Purchase of testing materials and test preparation programs for participants" as an allowable cost (34 CFR 643.30(b), 644.30(b)). This is the clearest regulatory statement that these grants can pay an outside test-prep vendor.
  - Student Support Services (SSS) must provide tutoring "directly or through other services provided by the institution".
  - **McNair is the exception.** 34 CFR 647.31(a) makes "test preparation" unallowable unless 647.30 expressly allows it, and 647.30 does not.
- **GEAR UP has the broadest menu for vendors.**
  - Its permissible activities include "college entrance examination preparation courses", tutoring, STEM programs, curriculum development, extended-day and summer programs, and "staff development". State grantees may also fund "professional development" for people working with the cohorts.
  - The statute says State grantees' activities "may be provided by service providers such as ... businesses" (20 U.S.C. 1070a-24(e)).
  - "In-kind or discounted program services" count toward GEAR UP's required 1:1 match (1070a-23(c)(4)). A vendor's discount can therefore help a grantee meet its match.
- **Hiring outside vendors is allowed under general ED and OMB rules, not program-specific ones.**
  - 34 CFR 75.708(e) says grantees "are authorized to contract, as needed, for supplies, equipment, and other services, in accordance with 2 CFR part 200".
  - Non-state grantees (colleges, nonprofits, school districts) must follow 2 CFR 200.318–200.327. Purchases at or below the micro-purchase threshold (FAR: $15,000; a grantee can self-certify up to $50,000) need no competition. Purchases up to the simplified acquisition threshold ($350,000) need quotes. Above that, a formal sealed bid or RFP is required.
  - State grantees (GEAR UP State) follow their own state procurement rules (2 CFR 200.317).
- **Incumbents get up to 15 extra "prior experience" (PE) points on top of a 100-point peer-review score.**
  - Basis: HEA §402A(c)(2)(A), 20 U.S.C. 1070a-11(c)(2)(A). The statute freezes the weight at its FY1994–1997 level.
  - The regulations score PE on outcome objectives for three designated project years and average the results.
  - Other incumbent advantages:
    - a statutory no-gap start date for "successful reapplicants" (1070a-11(c)(7));
    - higher maximum awards for incumbents in the notices (an incumbent may request its prior "base award amount", while new applicants are capped lower);
    - the general EDGAR rule giving continuation awards priority over new grants (34 CFR 75.253(c)).
  - GEAR UP has no PE points. Its State competitions give a small priority (0 or 2 points) to States that ran a successful State GEAR UP grant before Aug. 14, 2008.
- **Buying windows.** Neither the statute nor the program regulations set budget-period start dates. Budget periods are "usually ... not more than 12 months" (34 CFR 75.251), and each new one "begins on the day after the previous budget period ends" (75.253(d)). USAspending records show these new-award start dates:
  - TRIO (SSS, TS, EOC, UB, UBMS, VUB): mostly **Sept. 1**
  - McNair: **Oct. 1**
  - GEAR UP: **Sept. 1 or Oct. 1**
  - Some UB projects: June 1

### 1a. Key verbatim text by program

**Talent Search.** The statute, 20 U.S.C. 1070a-12(b), says "Any project assisted under this section shall provide—":
- "(1) connections to high quality academic tutoring services, to enable students to complete secondary or postsecondary courses;"
- "(3) assistance in preparing for college entrance examinations and completing college admission applications;"

Under 1070a-12(c), "(c) Permissible services. Any project assisted under this section may provide services such as—(1) academic tutoring, which may include instruction in reading, writing, study skills, mathematics, science, and other subjects; (2) personal and career counseling or activities; (3) information and activities designed to acquaint youth with the range of career options available to the youth; ... (5) workshops and counseling for families of students served".

The regulation 34 CFR 643.4(a) and (b) repeats both lists. 34 CFR 643.30 adds: "The cost principles that apply to the Talent Search program are in 2 CFR part 200, subpart E. Allowable costs include the following if they are reasonably related to the objectives of the project: ... (b) Purchase of testing materials and test preparation programs for participants. (c) Fees required for admission applications for postsecondary education, college entrance examinations, or alternative education examinations if—(1) A waiver of the fee is unavailable; and (2) The fee is paid by the grantee to a third party on behalf of a participant. (d) In-service training of project staff."

The section also allows tuition for up to two courses per year that are part of a "rigorous secondary school program of study", but only if the course is "taken through an accredited institution of higher education" (643.30(h)). Commercial course providers do not qualify for this item.

Unallowable costs (643.31) are "(a) Stipends and other forms of direct financial support for participants. (b) Application fees for financial aid. (c) Research not directly related to the evaluation or improvement of the project. (d) Construction, renovation, and remodeling of any facilities."

**Upward Bound, UBMS and VUB.** The statute, 20 U.S.C. 1070a-13(b), says "Any project assisted under this section shall provide—(1) academic tutoring to enable students to complete secondary or postsecondary courses, which may include instruction in reading, writing, study skills, mathematics, science, and other subjects; (2) advice and assistance in secondary and postsecondary course selection; (3) assistance in preparing for college entrance examinations and completing college admission applications;"

1070a-13(c): "Any project assisted under this section which has received funding for two or more years shall include, as part of the core curriculum in the next and succeeding years, instruction in mathematics through precalculus, laboratory science, foreign language, composition, and literature."

The regulations 34 CFR 645.11–645.15 repeat these and add structural requirements:
- 645.13(a)(1): regular UB "Must provide participants with a summer instructional component that is designed to simulate a college-going experience for participants, and an academic year component".
- 645.13(b): "Be six weeks in length" (unless the Secretary approves a shorter period).
- 645.14(a)(1), UBMS: "Intensive instruction in mathematics and science, including hands-on experience in laboratories, in computer facilities, and at field-sites".
- 645.15(a)–(b), VUB: "Provide intensive basic skills development ..." and "Provide short-term remedial or refresher courses for veterans who are high school graduates but who have delayed pursuing postsecondary education."

Note on 645.15: its heading reads "What additional services **may** Veterans Upward Bound projects provide?", but its text says "a Veterans Upward Bound project **must**—". The table treats these services as required, following the operative text.

UB allowable costs (645.40) include "(a) In-service training of project staff", "(k) Courses in English language instruction for project participants with limited proficiency in English", "(n) Purchase, lease, or rental of computer hardware, software, and other equipment, service agreements for such equipment, and supplies that support the delivery of services to participants", and "(p) Fees required for college admissions applications or entrance examinations" (if no waiver is available and the fee is paid to a third party).

645.40 has **no explicit "test preparation programs" line** like the one in TS and EOC. UB test-prep purchases would rest on the required service in 1070a-13(b)(3) and on the general 2 CFR 200 subpart E cost principles. That reading is UNVERIFIED: I found no ED statement on it in an allowed source.

**Student Support Services.** The statute, 20 U.S.C. 1070a-14(b)(1), requires "academic tutoring, directly or through other services provided by the institution, to enable students to complete postsecondary courses, which may include instruction in reading, writing, study skills, mathematics, science, and other subjects".

34 CFR 646.30 allowable costs include:
- "(a) Cost of remedial and special classes if—(1) These classes are not otherwise available at the grantee institution; (2) Are limited to eligible project participants; and (3) Project participants are not charged tuition for classes paid for by the project."
- "(c) In-service training of project staff."
- "(d) Activities of an academic or cultural nature, such as field trips, special lectures, and symposiums ..."
- "(g) Professional development travel for staff if directly related to the project's overall purpose and activities, except that these costs may not exceed four percent of total project salaries."

646.31(a) makes "Costs involved in recruiting students for enrollment at the institution" unallowable. 646.32(e)(1) adds that SSS funds may not support "activities reasonably available to the general student population".

**Educational Opportunity Centers.** The statute, 20 U.S.C. 1070a-16(b), says "An educational opportunity center assisted under this section may provide services such as— ... (4) assistance in preparing for college entrance examinations; ... (7) individualized personal, career, and academic counseling; (8) tutorial services; (9) career workshops and counseling".

34 CFR 644.30(b) makes "Purchase of testing materials and test preparation programs for participants" allowable, and 644.30(d) allows "In-service training of project staff."

644.31(a) makes "Tuition, fees, stipends, and other forms of direct financial support for participants" unallowable.

EOC is the TRIO program closest to the company's **workforce and reentry** line: it serves adults aged 19 and over, and offers career workshops and GED and reentry guidance. Note that "reentry" in the statute means *secondary school* reentry, not justice-system reentry. Justice-involved adults are not a named population; they could only fit as "other disconnected students" (1070a-16(b)(11)).

**McNair.** The statute, 20 U.S.C. 1070a-15(b), says "A project assisted under this section shall provide—(1) opportunities for research or other scholarly activities ...; (2) summer internships; (3) seminars and other educational activities designed to prepare students for doctoral study; (4) tutoring; (5) academic counseling; and (6) activities designed to assist students ... in securing admission to and financial assistance for enrollment in graduate programs."

The regulation 34 CFR 647.31(a) makes unallowable "Payment of tuition, stipends, test preparation and fees or any other form of student financial support to staff or participants not expressly allowed under § 647.30."

This plainly bars paying for GRE-style test prep for participants. Whether a GRE workshop could be bought as a "seminar ... designed to prepare students for doctoral study" under 1070a-15(b)(3) is UNVERIFIED. Given 647.31(a), it is risky.

**GEAR UP.**
- **Required activities.** 20 U.S.C. 1070a-24(a): "Each eligible entity receiving a grant under this division shall provide comprehensive mentoring, outreach, and supportive services to students participating in the programs under this division." The required list is financial-aid information, "Encouraging student enrollment in rigorous and challenging curricula and coursework", improving diploma and college-application and enrollment numbers, and (States) scholarships. 34 CFR 694.21 repeats it.
- **Permissible activities**, 1070a-24(b) and 34 CFR 694.22, all quoted in the table:
  - (b)(1) tutors and mentors
  - (b)(4) curricula, AP and IB
  - (b)(6) STEM tutoring
  - (b)(9) extended day, year, or summer programs
  - (b)(10)(B) after-school and summer tutoring
  - (b)(10)(C) summer jobs
  - (b)(10)(H) "skills assessments"
  - (b)(10)(J) "staff development"
  - (b)(11) "college entrance examination preparation courses"
- **State-only activities**, 1070a-24(c) and 694.23: (c)(2) "Providing professional development opportunities to individuals working with eligible cohorts of students"; (c)(7) standards and curricula alignment; (c)(8) credential and apprenticeship pathways.
- **First-year-of-college services** (694.24): "(g) Tutoring, mentoring, and supplemental academic support" and "(i) Career counseling and advising services".

### 1b. Is contracting with outside vendors allowed?

Yes. No program regulation prohibits procurement, and the general rules authorize it expressly.

- **34 CFR 75.708(e)** (EDGAR; applies to all of these discretionary grants): "Grantees that are not allowed to make subgrants under paragraph (b) of this section are authorized to contract, as needed, for supplies, equipment, and other services, in accordance with 2 CFR part 200, subpart D (2 CFR 200.317 through 200.326)."
  - Subgrants need statutory or notice authority (75.708(a)). None of the TRIO statutes authorizes them, so the TRIO route is a **procurement contract, not a subaward**.
- **GEAR UP State statute, 20 U.S.C. 1070a-24(e)** ("Allowable providers"): "In the case of eligible entities described in section 1070a–21(c)(1) of this title [States], the activities required by this section may be provided by service providers such as community-based organizations, schools, institutions of higher education, public and private agencies, nonprofit and philanthropic organizations, **businesses**, institutions and agencies sponsoring programs authorized under subpart 4, and other organizations the State determines appropriate."
- **GEAR UP Partnership membership.** A partnership "may include not less than two other community organizations or entities, such as **businesses**, professional organizations, State agencies ..." (1070a-21(c)(2)(B)). A company can therefore be a named partner and match contributor, not only a vendor.
- **GEAR UP services to private-school students** must be delivered "By employees of a public agency; or ... Through contract by the public agency with an individual, association, agency, or organization" (34 CFR 694.6(a)).
- **Cost principles.** Every TRIO allowable-cost section starts: "The cost principles that apply to the [program] are in 2 CFR part 200, subpart E." 2 CFR 200.459(a) makes "Costs of professional and consultant services rendered by persons who are members of a particular profession or possess a special skill and who are not officers or employees of the recipient or subrecipient" allowable "when reasonable in relation to the services rendered". Relevant factors include "(2) The necessity of contracting for the service, considering the recipient's or subrecipient's capability in the particular area" and "(6) Whether the service can be performed more economically by direct employment rather than contracting."
- **Contractor vs. subrecipient.** 2 CFR 200.331(b) defines a contractor as one that "(1) Provides the goods and services within normal business operations; (2) Provides similar goods or services to many different purchasers; (3) Normally operates in a competitive environment; (4) Provides goods or services that are ancillary to the implementation of a Federal program". This describes a test-prep or tutoring vendor.
- **Procurement standards** (2 CFR 200.317–200.320; ED adopts 2 CFR 200 at 2 CFR 3474.1):
  - States, which covers GEAR UP State grantees, "must follow the same policies and procedures it uses for procurements with non-Federal funds" (200.317).
  - All other recipients (colleges, nonprofits, school districts) "must follow the procurement standards in §§ 200.318 through 200.327" (200.317).
  - **Micro-purchase**: may be "awarded without soliciting competitive price or rate quotations if the recipient ... considers the price reasonable" (200.320(a)(1)(ii)). The FAR threshold is currently "$15,000" (48 CFR 2.101). A recipient "may self-certify a threshold up to $50,000 on an annual basis" (200.320(a)(1)(iv)).
  - **Simplified acquisition**: "price or rate quotations must be obtained from an adequate number of qualified sources" (200.320(a)(2)(i)), up to the FAR simplified acquisition threshold of "$350,000" (48 CFR 2.101).
  - **Above that**, a formal sealed bid or RFP is required. "Requests for proposals require public notice" (200.320(b)(2)(i)).
  - **Sole source** is allowed only in listed cases, such as "(2) The procurement transaction can only be fulfilled by a single source" (200.320(c)).
  - Practical implication: most TRIO projects average about $300K–$400K a year. A typical vendor purchase from one project is likely to fall in the micro-purchase or quote range rather than a formal RFP. That is an inference.
- **GEAR UP indirect cap.** For state or local government agencies the indirect rate is capped at "Eight percent of a modified total direct cost base" (34 CFR 694.11). This is not a vendor rule, but it affects how much of the budget is left for direct purchases.

## 2. Prior experience (PE) points

### Statutory basis

The correct cite is **HEA §402A(c)(2)(A), 20 U.S.C. 1070a-11(c)(2)(A)**:

> "In making grants under this division, the Secretary shall consider each applicant's prior experience of high quality service delivery, as determined under subsection (f), under the particular program for which funds are sought. The level of consideration given the factor of prior experience shall not vary from the level of consideration given such factor during fiscal years 1994 through 1997, except that grants made under section 1070a–18 of this title shall not be given prior experience consideration."

- **Ranking.** §402A(c)(3)(A), 1070a-11(c)(3)(A): "the Secretary shall award grants and contracts under this division in the order of the scores received by the application for such grant or contract in the peer review process required under paragraph (4) and adjusted for prior experience in accordance with paragraph (2) of this subsection." TRIO Training (1070a-17) and dissemination partnership grants (1070a-18) are excepted.
- **Outcome basis.** §402A(f)(1), 1070a-11(f)(1): "For competitions for grants under this division that begin on or after January 1, 2009, the Secretary shall determine an eligible entity's prior experience of high quality service delivery, as required under subsection (c)(2), based on the outcome criteria described in paragraphs (2) and (3)." Paragraph (3) lists the outcomes for each program.
- **Accurate PE awards and second review.** §402A(c)(8) requires guidance ensuring "prior experience points for high quality service delivery are awarded in an accurate and transparent manner", and a second-review process for scoring errors. This was added in 2008.

### Maximum points and calculation (identical structure in 643.20/22, 644.20/22, 645.30/32, 646.20/22, 647.20/22)

- **Maximum: 15 PE points**, added to a peer-review score with a maximum of 100. The regulation says: "The maximum total score for all the criteria in § 643.22 is 15 points" (643.20(a)(2)(ii); same in the other parts).
- **Who qualifies:**
  - TS, EOC, UB, UBMS, VUB and SSS: an application "to continue to serve substantially the same populations and campuses that the applicant is serving under an expiring project" (643.20(a)(2)(i), and the parallel sections).
  - McNair: "an applicant who has carried out a McNair project in the fiscal year immediately preceding the fiscal year for which the applicant is applying" (647.20(a)(2)(i)). If such an applicant submits several applications, PE applies only to the one continuing the existing campus project (647.20(a)(2)(iii)).
- **Averaging**: "The Secretary evaluates the PE of an applicant for each of the three project years that the Secretary designates in the Federal Register notice ... An applicant may earn up to 15 PE points for each of the designated project years for which annual performance report data are available. ... The final PE score is the average of the scores for the three project years assessed." (643.20(a)(2)(iii)–(v) and parallels.)
- **Data source**: the approved objectives of the expiring grant and its annual performance reports (APRs). The Secretary "May adjust a calculated PE score or decide not to award PE points if other information such as audit reports, site visit reports, and project evaluation reports indicates the APR data used to calculate PE are incorrect" (643.22(a)(3)).
- **Gates**: "The Secretary does not award PE points for a given year to an applicant that does not serve at least 90 percent of the approved number of participants" (643.22(b), and parallels). The 3-point "Number of participants" criterion requires serving 100% (643.22(c)).
- **Point allocation per year** (each criterion is met-or-exceeded the approved objective):
  - **TS** (643.22(d)), total 15:
    - number served: 3
    - secondary persistence: 3
    - graduation, regular diploma: 3
    - graduation, rigorous program: 1.5
    - postsecondary enrollment: 3
    - postsecondary completion: 1.5
  - **EOC** (644.22(d)), total 15:
    - number served: 3
    - secondary diploma or equivalent: 3
    - postsecondary enrollment: 5
    - financial aid applications: 2
    - college admission applications: 2
  - **UB and UBMS** (645.32(e)(1)), total 15:
    - number served: 3
    - GPA: 1.5
    - standardized tests: 1.5
    - retention and graduation: 3
    - rigorous program: 1.5
    - postsecondary enrollment: 3
    - postsecondary completion: 1.5
  - **VUB** (645.32(e)(2)), total 15:
    - number served: 3
    - "Academic improvement on standardized test": 3
    - program retention and completion: 3
    - postsecondary enrollment: 3
    - postsecondary completion: 3
  - **SSS** (646.22(e)), total 15:
    - number served: 3
    - retention: 4
    - good academic standing: 4
    - for four-year institutions, degree completion: 4
    - for two-year institutions, completion: 2, and transfer: 2
  - **McNair** (647.22(e)), total 15:
    - number served: 3
    - research activities: 3
    - graduate enrollment: 3
    - continued graduate enrollment: 4
    - doctoral attainment: 2
- **Sales relevance.** Standardized-test improvement is a scored PE outcome in UB and UBMS (1.5 points a year) and in VUB (3 points a year). This links test-prep and tutoring vendors directly to an incumbent's re-competition score. Treat this as a selling point, not a legal finding.

### How the notices applied PE in recent competitions

- **SSS FY2025** (89 FR 35080, May 1, 2024): "the Secretary will award up to 15 prior experience points to applicants that have conducted an SSS Program project within the last three Federal government fiscal years".
- **UB, UBMS, VUB and McNair FY2022** (86 FR 71460; 87 FR 23170; 87 FR 24537; 87 FR 13280): PE for "budget periods 2017-18, 2018-19, 2019-20, and 2020-21". That is **four** years listed, while the regulations say three. The notices do not explain the difference; see open questions.
- **TS FY2026** (91 FR 13826, Mar. 23, 2026, issued by DOL ETA on ED's behalf): "The Secretary will award prior experience points to applicants that have conducted a Talent Search project during budget periods 2022-23, 2023-24, and 2024-25, based on their documented experience."
- **EOC FY2026** (91 FR 16930, Apr. 3, 2026): "As required by statute and program regulations, the Secretary will award prior experience points to applicants that have conducted an EOC project. Such points will be awarded based on the applicant's performance during second, third, and fourth years of the performance period."

### Other rules that favor incumbents

1. **No funding gap for successful reapplicants.** §402A(c)(7), 1070a-11(c)(7): "The Secretary, in the case of an entity that is continuing to operate a successful program under this division, shall ensure that the start-up date for a new grant or contract for such program immediately follows the termination of the preceding grant or contract so that no interruption of funding occurs for such successful reapplicants." The same paragraph requires notice of application status "at least 8 months prior to the expiration of the grant".
2. **Higher award ceilings for incumbents** (notice-level):
   - SSS FY2025: "For applicants currently receiving an SSS Program grant, the maximum award amount is the greater of (a) $272,364 or (b) 100 percent of the applicant's base award amount for FY 2024." New applicants are capped at $272,364.
   - UB FY2022: new applicants capped at "$287,537". For a current grantee, "the maximum award amount is equal to the applicant's base award amount for FY 2021".
   - McNair FY2022: new applicants capped at $261,888; incumbents get their FY2021 base award.
3. **Continuations come first.** 34 CFR 75.253(c): "the Secretary gives priority to continuation awards over new grants."
4. **Past performance in selection.** 34 CFR 75.217(d)(3)(ii): the Secretary may consider information "Concerning the applicant's performance and use of funds under a previous award under any Department program". This can cut either way.
5. **GEAR UP has no PE points, but has a statutory priority for older State grantees.**
   - 20 U.S.C. 1070a-21(b)(3)(A) requires priority for States that "on the day before August 14, 2008, carried out successful educational opportunity programs under this division" and "have a prior, demonstrated commitment to early intervention". 34 CFR 694.19 implements it.
   - The FY2025 State NIA scored this as "Competitive Preference Priority 1—Successful State GEAR UP grant prior to August 14, 2008 (0 or 2 points)" (89 FR 91715).
   - The June 11, 2025 correction (90 FR 24598) removed the other priorities and left "one competitive preference priority". I infer it is this one, since the correction deletes Priorities 2–4 by name.
   - **Counter-incumbent rule:** the FY2025 State NIA restricted eligibility: "Per congressional direction in Senate Report 118-84 (Pub. L. 118-47), only States without an active State GEAR UP grant, or States that have an active State GEAR UP grant that is scheduled to end prior to October 1, 2025, are eligible".
   - GEAR UP grantees awarded on or after Aug. 14, 2008 that win a new grant must keep serving students from the prior grant (34 CFR 694.25; 1070a-22(d)(1)(C)).

### Recent changes

- **Statute.** The PE text (§402A(c)(2)(A)) and the outcome criteria (§402A(f)) date from the Higher Education Opportunity Act, Pub. L. 110-315 (Aug. 14, 2008). Cornell's amendment notes show no later change to these provisions; the latest listed amendment year is 2009.
- **Regulations.** The PE sections were last amended at 75 FR 65712 et seq. (Oct. 26, 2010). eCFR version history shows the only 2025–2026 changes to parts 643, 644, 645 and 647 are to the participant-eligibility sections 643.3 and 645.3 (90 FR 504, Jan. 3, 2025, and a further change effective 2026-07-01). Part 646 is unchanged since the 2016 baseline. Part 694 was last changed in 2021.
- **Notices (FY2026, under DOL administration).** The PE mechanics are unchanged, but the FY2026 TS and EOC competitions changed the economics:
  - TS: "All projects must serve a minimum of 500 participants annually and have a per participant cost of no more than $500".
  - EOC: "a minimum of 850 participants annually and ... a per participant cost of no more than $280".
  - Each notice allows one Governor-designated "state-level applicant" ($10,000,000 TS; $3,000,000 EOC). The new priorities include "Expanding Access to Talent Marketplaces".
  - Low per-participant costs push grantees toward scalable, low-unit-cost services. That is an inference about sales positioning.

## 3. Buying windows (budget-period start dates)

**What the law says.** No program statute or regulation (34 CFR parts 643–647, 694) fixes a calendar start date. The rules are general:
- Project periods: TRIO is "five years" (643.5, 644.5, 645.34, 646.5, 647.5; 1070a-11(b)(2)). GEAR UP is "six years" or "seven years" (1070a-21(b)(2)).
- Budget periods: "The Secretary usually approves a budget period of not more than 12 months, even if the project has a multi-year project period" (34 CFR 75.251(a)).
- Continuations: "The new budget period begins on the day after the previous budget period ends" (34 CFR 75.253(d)(2)).
- The Federal Register NIAs reviewed give only "Project Period: Up to 60 months" (TRIO) or "Either 72 months or 84 months" (GEAR UP), with no start date.
- The FY2026 TS and EOC application instructions on grants.gov may state an anticipated start date. Those were not consulted (outside the allowed sources): UNVERIFIED.

**Practical buying calendar.** This is an inference from the dates above plus program structure, not law:
- **TRIO (SSS, TS, EOC, UB):** new-year budgets start about Sept. 1. Purchasing for academic-year tutoring and test prep follows award notices in late summer.
- **UB summer components:** regular UB and UBMS must run a six-week summer instructional component. That demand is planned and bought in spring, near the end of the budget year.
- **McNair:** budget years start Oct. 1. Its summer research internships are mid-budget-year.
- **GEAR UP:** budget years start Sept. 1 or Oct. 1 depending on cohort, so look up each grant's start date.

## 4. GEAR UP match and scholarships (brief)

### Match

20 U.S.C. 1070a-23(b)(1)(A) requires the grantee to provide, "from State, local, institutional, or private funds, not less than 50 percent of the cost of the program, which matching funds may be provided in cash or in kind and may be accrued over the full duration of the grant award period, except that the eligible entity shall make substantial progress towards meeting the matching requirement in each year". 34 CFR 694.7(b) defines this as "one dollar of non-Federal contributions for every one dollar of Federal funds obligated for the project".

What counts toward the match (1070a-23(c)):
- non-Federal student financial assistance and scholarship contributions;
- waived tuition, fees, room or board;
- "documented, targeted, long-term mentoring and counseling provided by volunteers or paid staff of nonschool organizations, including **businesses** ...";
- "other resources recognized by the Secretary, including equipment and supplies, cash contributions from non-Federal sources, transportation expenses, **in-kind or discounted program services**, indirect costs, and facility usage."

A vendor's documented discount, or donated services, can count toward the grantee's match. This is worth stating in sales materials, subject to the grantee's documentation rules.

Partnership waivers (Partnerships only; 1070a-23(b)(2); 34 CFR 694.8–694.9):
- up to 75% of the match for up to two years for a disaster-type hardship;
- up to 50% for ongoing economic hardship;
- 2:1 matching of scholarship-fund contributions;
- up to 70% for partnerships with three or fewer IHEs that serve high-poverty schools and LEAs.

Partnership grants are capped at "$800" times the number of students served per year (34 CFR 694.1(a)). The FY2025 State NIA capped State awards at "$5,000,000 for a single budget period of 12 months".

### Scholarships

- **States must run a scholarship program** (1070a-25(a)(1); 694.12(a)(1)). Partnerships "may" (1070a-25(a)(2)).
- **States must split grant funds between services and scholarships.** 1070a-25(b)(1): a State "shall use not less than 25 percent and not more than 50 percent of the grant funds for activities described in section 1070a–24 ..., with the remainder of such funds to be used for a scholarship program". The Secretary may allow more than 50% for activities if the State shows "another means of providing the students with the financial assistance" (1070a-25(b)(2)).
  - **Implication:** in a State grant without that exception, at most half the Federal dollars go to services the company could sell. The rest is reserved for scholarships.
- **Minimum scholarship**: not less than the minimum Federal Pell Grant (1070a-25(d); 694.14(a)(2)).
- **Reserve and portability**: States must "hold in reserve" the minimum scholarship times the estimated number of eligible students (1070a-25(e)(1); 694.14(c)). Unused funds can be redistributed, or must be returned, after six years (694.16).
